CC0 1.0 Pilot

Empirical Public-Domain Biblical Lexicon

Clean-room Greek, Hebrew & Aramaic lexicography from primary texts

Why This Pilot Exists

Modern scholarly biblical lexicons (BDAG, HALOT, DCH) are copyrighted and restricted, while classic public-domain lexicons (Strong’s, BDB, Thayer’s) are over a century old and often rely on outdated comparative etymology and root fallacies.

This project tests an empirical, clean-room alternative: deriving senses, grammatical frames, semantic domains, and collocations strictly from primary ancient sentences (Nestle 1904 Greek NT and Westminster Leningrad Codex Hebrew/Aramaic OT), with every sense backed by verified verse citations and released into the Public Domain (CC0 1.0).

Expert Evaluation Goal

Tier 1 covers every word occurring in Jonah (OT) and Philippians (NT)—696 lemmas across both testaments—to validate whether empirical quality justifies scaling to the remaining 13,346 biblical lemmas.

Pipeline & Clean-Room Guardrails

  1. Primary-Text Ingestion (01_ingest.py): Loads 607,255 tokens from Nestle 1904 (PD/CC0) and WLC (PD). Third-party glosses (Macula Hebrew CC BY, Dodson Greek CC0) are isolated in benchmark_hidden and never shown to the AI.
  2. Stratified Evidence Packets (02_export.py): Packs pending lemmas with up to 25–40 primary-text sentences marking target occurrences with 【...】.
  3. Empirical Synthesis (GEMINI.md rules): Prohibits imitating copyrighted lexicons, guessing from 3-letter roots, or fabricating references; requires honest low confidence on ambiguous rare words.
  4. Deterministic Verification (06_verify.py): Validates every cited verse against tokens, checks Hebrew verb stem coverage against morph_note, lints clean-room rules, and scores alignment against benchmark_hidden.
  5. CC0 Publication (05_publish.py): Exports clean public-domain JSON/JSONL deliverables with zero third-party gloss leakage.

Pilot Findings & Automated Audit Notes

Key Pilot Observation: Verb Stem Sampling

All 696 Tier 1 entries passed citation, schema, and clean-room rule checks (0 hallucinated verses, 0 lexicon leaks). However, automated stem verification flagged 7 high-frequency Hebrew verbs (e.g., עָשָׂה, בּוֹא, הָיָה, שׁוּב) where uniform 40-verse sampling missed a less-frequent stem like Niphal or Hophal. Stratifying future evidence packets by verb stem will fix this before scaling to Tiers 2–4.
